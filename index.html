    Business review & market analysis

    A Phoenix concierge panel, priced as a practice.

    Seventeen years, roughly 400 prepaid members, $257,526 of seller's discretionary earnings, and a $435,000 ask. The economics work or fail on a single question: who sits in the chair after closing.

    Target · 17-yr concierge family practice, Phoenix AZ Listing · BizBuySell 2506062 Prepared for · Mindbody Associates
    The listing

    The headline numbers

    Membership is effectively the whole business. Four hundred members at $1,800 is about $720K of the $795K reported, leaving roughly $75K of ancillary revenue.

    The practice is priced below the normal multiple range for a business this size, and that discount is doing work. It is compensation for a risk the listing does not price explicitly, which is the subject of sections three and four.

    Operating profile

    What the practice actually is

    Issue one

    The margin is a salary

    Seller's discretionary earnings include the owner's compensation. A replacement family physician in Phoenix costs roughly $230,000 to $280,000 in total compensation.

    Without a physician who will actually practice in it, the headline SDE is not earnings. It is the price of the labor the buyer now has to go buy.

    Issue two

    Members pay for a doctor, not a clinic

    Seventeen years of relationship is the entire asset, and it walks out at closing. Fixed costs do not fall with member count: rent, the NP, front office, and EHR hold flat, so every lost member comes almost entirely out of margin.

    A panel that is 65% over 60 erodes further on its own, through mortality and Medicare transitions, regardless of how good the service is. At the 30% row the practice services its own purchase price slowly and does nothing else.

    Market context

    The segment is growing and the data is noisy

    Treat the direction as reliable and the absolute market size as marketing. What is solid is the demand driver: a documented primary care shortage, tripling physician participation in retainer models, and a still-fragmented cottage industry of a few thousand practices nationally.

    Pricing

    The underpricing claim checks out

    At $1,800 the practice sits below the floor of the national band, not merely below the listing's quoted range. The headroom is real.

    The timing is the problem. A 39% to 94% increase in the same year the founding physician leaves is the single most reliable way to lose the panel. The listing's claimed +$280K to $480K of repricing upside assumes full retention through that increase. Realistic capture is 40% to 60% of it, phased across two years, and it belongs in year two at the earliest.

    Geography

    Strong metro, younger city

    The metro tailwind is among the strongest in the country, but Phoenix proper is younger than the Valley around it. Retiree concentration sits in Scottsdale, Sun City, Mesa, and Surprise. A hospital-campus location draws from a wider radius, which helps, but this is not Sun City demographics and should not be modeled as if it were.

    Competitive position

    The squeeze is on the middle

    Positioned at $1,800 the practice sits in the segment under pressure from both directions. The strategic move is up, not sideways, and that move needs a physician in place first.

    Projections

    Base case: owner-physician, measured repricing

    Against a $435,000 purchase, payback lands at roughly 2.2 years.

    Scenarios

    Three paths, two endpoints

    The aggressive path reaches nearly the same endpoint through a worse first year and real reputational exposure in a referral-dependent market. The downside case is the one that matters.

    The scenario that decides it

    $120K of SDE before physician compensation, against a $250K salary, is cash-flow negative for three years. That outcome requires nothing unusual to happen. It requires only that attrition lands at the high end of the normal range and that Mindbody Associates has to hire.

    What is genuinely attractive

    Strategic read

    A panel, not a practice

    For a cognitive and brain health platform, 400 prepaid patients with 65% over 60 is not primarily a family medicine practice. It is a screening population, a GUIDE Model eligible cohort, and a trial recruitment base, in a state where a footprint may be wanted regardless.

    Built from scratch, acquiring 400 engaged seniors costs well more than $435,000 in marketing spend and years of time. Valued as a panel rather than as a practice, the price looks materially different, and so does the attrition risk, because a panel used for screening and recruitment does not need every member to stay a concierge subscriber.

    Is operating a family medicine practice something Mindbody Associates wants, or is this the cheapest available route to a Phoenix patient base? The answer changes both the valuation method and the diligence list.
